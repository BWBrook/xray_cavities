# Energy stored in ambient gas connected to candidate axial fronts

The four production runs use a stretched 960 × 960 × 1,014 Cartesian grid, a single fluid with γ = 5/3, and twin 0.1c jets. Labelled bipolar power is $2\times10^{45}$ erg/s (HI) or $2\times10^{43}$ erg/s (LO) in the Stewart cluster (002_003) or group (002_031) atmosphere. The selected single-precision PLUTO outputs lie within 0.0009 Myr of 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 Myr. G-HI contributes through 40 Myr to this selection and through 47 Myr to the annual axial measurements. Each estimate compares the evolved cells with those same cells at time zero. Percentages use the cumulative signed gas energy delivered through the injector, $D$, including pressure work and exchange through its stationary part. Each side uses the same bipolar denominator, so the side fractions add. [Grid and environments](four_case_fluid_snapshot_census_2026-08-25.md) · [Delivery measurement](native_delivery_restatement_2026-11-21.md)

At 30 Myr, ambient cells with excess entropy connected to the axial front have gained energy equal to **63.0% of delivery in C-HI, 54.3% in G-HI and 69.4% in G-LO**. Thermal energy contributes the largest share in all three. At 30 Myr in G-LO, doubling the selection thresholds gives 54.0% and halving them gives 77.0%. The sole bilateral C-LO selection gained 20.6% at 10 Myr. Pressure and density both rise across the sampled axial edge on both sides in **C-HI at 10 Myr and G-HI at 10, 20 and 30 Myr**. In both low-power runs the sampled edges fall inside the projected lobe outline. Whether the surrounding atmosphere has a bow shock there remains open. ## Where the jumps were measured

The axial finder averages pressure, density and axial velocity over a tube of radius 2 kpc. It takes the outer edge of the last contiguous run of planes with outward motion and a thermodynamic disturbance, then compares the two axial planes immediately inside that edge with the two immediately outside. All 30 sides in the 15 accepted bilateral selections pass the position, jump-sampling and three-output propagation gates. Eleven sides have pressure and density ratios that both admit a compressive Rankine–Hugoniot solution. Eight of these sides form the four bilateral pairs above. Claude compared each axial edge with the farthest tracer-bearing pixel on three central sightlines in the side-on projection. Of the 19 sides lacking one or both jumps, **18 lie behind the projected tracer tip and one coincides with it**. All 12 low-power sides (C-LO at 10 Myr and G-LO at 10–50 Myr) lie 1.08–4.71 kpc behind. In G-LO at 10–50 Myr the ten inner-to-outer pressure ratios are 0.60–0.90 and the density ratios 0.018–0.840. Eight density ratios are at most 0.35. Those measurements describe the region near the projected lobe head. A shock farther out in the atmosphere could escape this test. All 30 accepted edges advance at Mach 1.41–16.38 relative to the **initial** sound speed. On the 11 sides with both jumps, the measured pressure ratio is 4.7–49.4% of the normal-shock prediction $(5M^2-1)/4$ at that propagation Mach number. At G-HI/20 the jump-derived Mach ranges are 2.24–2.54 from pressure and 1.81–2.19 from density, while propagation gives 8.46–8.54. At the measured density ratios the two inner pressures exceed the adiabatic predictions from the outer samples by 74% and 78%. These compressive jumps support a shock interpretation locally. The strength of a curved or smeared shock requires the normal speed relative to the evolving upstream gas and samples that resolve both states. ## What the energy measures

The native selection starts with compressed ambient cells (tracer at or below 0.001) near the candidate axial front, follows 26-connected cells with an excess of $\ln(p/\rho^\gamma)$ above the local initial atmosphere, and checks extent and boundary contact. Every selected cell contributes at full volume. The columns named `downstream` in the archived tables contain this ambient selection. In the two low-power runs its physical relation to an atmospheric shock remains unresolved. Halving or doubling the excess thresholds changes the bilateral total by less than 0.005 percentage points of $D$ in G-HI, by up to 7.4 points in C-HI, 12.1 points in C-LO/10 and 20.7 points in G-LO. That last figure is the change from the primary to the doubled thresholds at 40 Myr (61.6% to 40.9%). NAT-03 remains provisional. The archived three-dimensional integrals cover the whole domain and give the ambient parent and remainder for each component. Boundary inflow can contribute to that remainder. Intersections with R100 and R300 and the selected mass change needed to shift the gravitational reference were not archived. The gravitational term uses the initial potential with zero at the centre. [Binary selection and sensitivity](q029_binary_native_refresh_2026-09-06.md) · [Component integrals](../../scripts/science/snapshot_shock_energy.py)

The final column of the table is part of the thermal change. It is the stored excess above a compression or expansion starting with the gas initially at each position,

$$
E_{\rm excess} = \sum_{\rm selected} \frac{p-p_0(\rho/\rho_0)^\gamma}{\gamma-1}\,V .
$$

The complementary same-cell adiabatic term can be negative. In C-LO/10 it is −3.8% of $D$, which reconciles the 19.2% excess with the 15.4% thermal gain. Gas advected from another radius can carry a different initial entropy, and numerical entropy updates can change the stored result. The archived inventories provide neither the upstream entropy of each parcel nor the mass processed over time. They therefore leave the cumulative energy dissipated in bow shocks, internal shocks, and its split from reversible compression undetermined. A sum of these snapshots would repeatedly count gas remaining in the selected volume.
