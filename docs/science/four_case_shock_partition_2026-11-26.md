# Energy stored in ambient gas behind candidate bow fronts

The four production runs use a stretched 960 × 960 × 1,014 Cartesian grid, a single fluid with γ = 5/3, and twin 0.1c jets. Labelled bipolar power is $2\times10^{45}$ erg/s (HI) or $2\times10^{43}$ erg/s (LO) in the Stewart cluster (002_003) or group (002_031) atmosphere. The selected single-precision PLUTO outputs lie within 0.0009 Myr of 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 Myr. G-HI contributes through 40 Myr to this selection and through 47 Myr to the annual axial measurements. Each estimate compares the evolved cells with those same cells at time zero. Percentages use the cumulative signed gas energy delivered through the injector, $D$, including pressure work and exchange through its stationary part. Each side uses the same bipolar denominator, so the side fractions add. At 30 Myr the connected ambient region selected behind the candidate front stores **63.0% of delivery in C-HI, 54.3% in G-HI and 69.4% in G-LO**. Thermal energy dominates all three. The axial jump measurements distinguish these cases. G-HI shows pressure and density jumps on both sides. C-HI shows pressure jumps on both sides, and G-LO shows neither jump on either side. C-LO has no bilateral 30-Myr estimate. The measured selection can describe where the simulated atmosphere gained energy even when the cause remains ambiguous. ## Which fronts have physical jump evidence?

Of the 15 accepted bilateral energy selections, **four have pressure and density jumps that each permit a compressive Rankine–Hugoniot Mach number on both sides**. These are C-HI/10 and G-HI/10, /20 and /30. All eight axial tracks also move outward. The Mach estimates differ. For example, at G-HI/20, pressure gives 2.24–2.54, density gives 1.81–2.19, and propagation against the initial sound speed gives 8.46–8.54. The G-HI/20 downstream pressures exceed the adiabatic predictions from the immediately upstream samples by 74% and 78% at the measured density ratios, using $(p_d/p_u)/(\rho_d/\rho_u)^\gamma-1$. These relative contrasts describe the gas on the axis and carry no energy units. The axial test samples the mean across one and two cells either side of the candidate edge. A curved or smeared front can escape that stencil. The four cases with both jumps supply evidence for an ambient bow shock near the axis. Extending that identification to the entire connected volume requires a three-dimensional test. In G-LO, all ten side-age samples have downstream/upstream pressure ratios of 0.60–0.90 and density ratios of 0.018–0.840. In C-LO/10 the only compressive ratio is a pressure jump of 1.0076 on one side. At C-LO/40 the positive-side edge actually moves inward at Mach 1.058. ## What the energy measures

The regional measurement includes downstream gas. Its native selection starts with compressed ambient cells near the axial front, follows 26-connected cells with an excess of $\ln(p/\rho^\gamma)$ above the local initial atmosphere, and checks extent and boundary contact. Every selected cell contributes at full volume. Halving or doubling the excess thresholds changes the bilateral total by less than 0.005 percentage points of $D$ in G-HI, by up to 7.4 points in C-HI, 12.1 points in C-LO/10 and 20.7 points in G-LO. These are changes in which gas is selected. NAT-03 remains provisional against a cellwise no-jet comparison. The three-dimensional mask was summed over the whole domain. Its intersections with R100 and R300 were not retained. The table gives the whole-domain ambient parent and the remainder for each component. Boundary inflow can contribute to that remainder. The gravitational term uses the initial potential with zero at the centre. The selected cell mass change required to change its zero was not archived. [Binary selection and sensitivity](q029_binary_native_refresh_2026-09-06.md) · [Component integrals](../../scripts/science/snapshot_shock_energy.py)

The final column of the table is part of the thermal change. It is the stored excess above a compression or expansion starting with the gas initially at each position,

$$
E_{\rm excess} = \sum_{\rm selected} \frac{p-p_0(\rho/\rho_0)^\gamma}{\gamma-1}\,V .
$$

The complementary same-cell adiabatic term can be negative. In C-LO/10 it is −3.8% of $D$, which reconciles the 19.2% excess with the 15.4% thermal gain. Gas advected from another radius can carry a different initial entropy, and numerical entropy updates can change the stored result. The archived inventories provide neither the upstream entropy of each parcel nor the mass processed over time. They therefore leave the cumulative energy dissipated in bow shocks, internal shocks, and its split from reversible compression undetermined. A sum of these snapshots would repeatedly count gas remaining in the selected volume.
