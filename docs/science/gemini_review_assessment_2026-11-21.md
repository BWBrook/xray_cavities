## What to read from the entropy experiment

Entropy reconstruction can increase or decrease a cell’s energy. For an idealised equal-volume, equal-pressure mixture with a 100:1 density contrast, advecting entropy density and reconstructing pressure gives 7.1483 times the initial pressure. Mixing gas with different velocities contributes a negative kinetic term. The positive thermal term follows for equal pressures. Sign and magnitude in a production cell also depend on its actual pressures, velocities, numerical fluxes and stage history. This calculation supplies a mechanism to test.

Read the positive and negative entropy changes separately, with their Runge–Kutta weights, and report pressure-floor corrections alongside them. Measure jet-cone delivery and the stationary part of the injector separately in each arm. A change in delivered power is itself a result. The counters group radii from 0.75 to 5 kpc and tracer fractions from 0.005 to 0.5. They cannot attribute the 0.75–0.85 kpc shell residual to a particular operation or separate contact heating from shear loss within one cell. That 2.25% shell deficit is a saved-state flux balance. Both approved pairs are high-power and span 10–12 Myr. Low-power contact heating and a systematic correction out to 50 Myr remain untested. ## What the X-ray data establish

The near-ambient emissivity assertion is in NAT-05. XR-02 remains open. A passive tracer fraction measures jet mass fraction. Inferring a volume fraction requires phase densities, and band emissivity also depends on density structure and temperature. Assuming a pure-jet to ambient density ratio of 0.001 gives a jet volume fraction of 0.50025 at tracer mass fraction 0.001. The simulation does not measure those two subcell phases independently. Raising the tracer cut from 0.001 to 0.005 retains 87.4–93.1% of lobe volume and 85.9–92.6% of enthalpy over the 19 retained case-ages.
